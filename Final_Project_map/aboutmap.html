Introduction, Motivation, Objectives

This Web Map serves as an interactive guide to parks and hiking / biking trails in La Crosse Wisconsin and the surrounding counties.
I wanted to make this map because I have personally found that finding information on new places to hike can be daunting, and require searching through multiple websites. I believe this map can consolidate otherwise scattered information for avid enjoyers of the outdoors, or those in the La Crosse area who seek to get more into outdoor activity, but do not know where to look

Data and Methodology

Spatial data was acquired from the Wisconsin DNR GIS Open Data Portal, the Minnesota Geospatial Commons, and La Crosse County GIS resources, and Tailforks.
For trail data acquired from opensource geospatial data, the processing involved downloading and importing shpfiles in to ArcGIS pro, selecting significant trails from each dataset, modifying the attribute table to include fields that matched my desired layers, and exporting as geojson files. For the trails downloaded from trailforks, each trail was individually downloaded as a KML file and imported into a feature class using the KML to Layer tool.
For the amenity score thematic layer, I based my rating primarily on the availability of parking and restrooms. I also took into account campsite availability and quality of the trails. This information is based on the point data for restrooms and parking from Trailforks, park and trail reviews from AllTrails, and personal experience. For example, the state parks are all rated highly because they have multiple parking spaces, restrooms, and campgrounds. Municipal parks are rated slightly lower because they have parking and restrooms, but no camping. More primitive parks such as Coulee Experimental Forest have the lowest rating, as it only has one parking area and no restrooms.

Project Notes

I believe that the simplicity of this project is a big strength. I originally wanted to add several more factors such as boat launches, campsites, and beaches, but time contraints and difficulty finding open source data led me to deciding on 2 thematic layers and 2 geometry layers. However, I think this makes the map very concise and to the point. The 2 thematic layers that I currently have work well with each other, and I didn't experience any conflicts or difficulty with layers loading. Finding data for trails in lesser known state parks was much more difficult than I expected, and I ran into data loss while I was digitizing trail data for the last several parks. Before the final submission, I plan to re-digitize the trails that I lost, and add a point layer for trailheads with a popup.
